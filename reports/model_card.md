# Model card - OnTime late-delivery risk model

_Generated by `make report` from run outputs. Format follows Mitchell et al., "Model Cards for Model
Reporting"._

## 1. Model details
- **Task:** binary classification at order approval time: will the order be delivered on a calendar
  date after the promised date?
- **Primary model:** Logistic regression. Alternatives trained and evaluated: logistic regression, EBM,
  LightGBM, random forest, plus a prior-rate and a hand-written rule baseline.
- **Primary-model selection:** pre-registered rule - highest PR-AUC on the validation slice that was
  never used for tuning (0.174 vs a 0.076 base rate there).
- **Training window:** 2016-09-15 to 2018-01-31 (50,603 orders). Models are fit on
  train only; validation was used for tuning and calibration; the test set was evaluated once.
- **Version / reproducibility:** git commit `c8b734b`, seed 42, trained 2026-10-14.
  Key libraries: scikit-learn 1.9.1, lightgbm 4.7.0, interpret 0.7.8, shap 0.51.0, duckdb 1.5.6, pandas 3.0.6, streamlit 1.65.0.
- **Inputs:** 37 features known at approval (distance and geography, promise and
  handling windows, calendar, order composition, payment, and leak-safe seller/route/network history).
  Forbidden: carrier and delivery timestamps, review data, anything recorded after approval.
- **Outputs:** a risk score (calibrated probability for the deployed calibrator), a within-day rank
  and a risk band, plus top reasons that add and reduce risk.

## 2. Intended use
- **Primary use:** rank each day's orders by late-delivery risk so a capacity-limited operations team
  can act on the riskiest first (expedite, nudge a seller, message the customer *before* the
  promised date).
- **Intended users:** operations and seller-management analysts.
- **Out of scope:** automated penalties on sellers; individual customer decisions; any use outside
  e-commerce parcel delivery; use of absolute probabilities for pricing or SLAs; other
  marketplaces or periods without re-validation.

## 3. Data
- **Source:** Olist Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), 9 relational tables,
  about one hundred thousand orders across 2016-2018.
- **Population:** delivered orders with a valid approval time, approved before the censoring cutoff
  (2018-08-20); 94,937 orders after filtering (funnel in `reports/data_quality.md`).
- **Target:** `is_late` = delivered date later than the promised date (dates, not timestamps).
- **Splits (by approval time):** train 5.6% late, validation 12.6% late,
  test 4.5% late. The late rate differs substantially between periods.

## 4b. Stage 2: re-scoring at carrier handover
- **What it is:** a second model that re-scores each order when the seller hands it to the carrier,
  adding the order's own handover timing, the seller's recent handover punctuality and a destination
  backlog (earlier orders still in transit). Customer delivery date and reviews are never used.
- **Selection:** LightGBM, chosen by validation PR-AUC (0.255 at approval vs
  0.403 at handover on validation); scored on the test months once.
- **Test result (same orders):** PR-AUC 0.395 [0.365, 0.422] vs 0.093 at approval; ROC-AUC
  0.826 vs 0.701; lift at 10% capacity 4.6x [4.13, 5.05] vs 2.4x.
- **Time to act:** handover happens a median 1.2 days after approval; late orders have
  a median 10 days left before the promised date.
- **Caveat:** the test months were already seen once for stage 1; stage 2 is a separate, pre-declared
  model on the same months and needs fresh data for confirmation. Ranking only (no calibration).

## 8. Explainability
- Exact additive decompositions in log-odds: logistic regression (coefficients), EBM (term
  scores), LightGBM (TreeSHAP); verified by unit tests to sum to the model output.
- Across the three explanation methods, global importance agrees most between LightGBM and the EBM (rank correlation 0.78); 26 of 32 numeric features have the same effect direction in at least two models.
- Explanations are about the *model*, not about causes. Calendar features are used by every model
  and mostly encode which part of the year the training data covered.

## 9. Ethical considerations and fairness
- **Seller impact.** A late-risk score could be misused to penalise sellers. New sellers are
  protected by Bayesian shrinkage toward the platform average (pseudo-count 20 orders), but
  the model still carries seller history; it should inform support, not sanctions.
- **Regional disparity.** Delivery risk is higher for some regions (see slices). Treating the
  *customer's* region as a risk factor could lead to worse service for those customers if used to
  deprioritise them; the intended use is the opposite (extra attention to risky orders).
- **Data limits.** Orders that were cancelled or never delivered are not in the training population.
- **Privacy.** The dataset is anonymised; the app shows order and seller identifiers only.

## 10. Caveats and recommendations
1. Re-validate before any use outside the 2016-2018 Olist data; confirm stage 2 on new data.
2. Monitor weekly: rolling PR-AUC, calibration, and feature drift; retrain on a schedule.
3. Present risk **bands and rank** to users, not exact percentages.
4. Measure the intervention success rate with a randomized pilot before relying on the policy
   simulation (at 10% daily capacity and an assumed 30% intervention success, about 74 late orders prevented (6.7% of all late) vs 33 for random selection; ceiling with perfect ranking 306).
5. Treat the review-score effect (0.6 to 2.0 stars) as an association.
