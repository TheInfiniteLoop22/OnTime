## Phase 3 - EDA + business impact (2026-10-14)

**Goal:** understand the target before modelling, quantify why lateness matters, and finalize

**Acceptance (BUILD_PLAN):** notebooks run top-to-bottom via `nbconvert --execute`; impact numbers
are in `impact.json` with CIs; wording says "adjusted association", not "causal effect".

**Built**
- `src/ontime/analysis/eda.py` - derived analysis columns, Wilson-CI rate tables, 9 report figures.
- `src/ontime/analysis/impact.py` - review de-duplication, descriptive stats, Mann-Whitney,
  bootstrap CIs, clustered OLS, seller fixed effects, ordered logit, dose-response, review-timing
  analysis; `run_impact()` writes `reports/impact.json`.
- `notebooks/01_eda.ipynb` (25 cells, 8 questions) and `notebooks/02_impact_analysis.ipynb`
  (23 cells). Every inference cell is computed from data (no hand-typed numbers).
- `make impact`, `make notebooks`; `tests/test_impact.py` (8 tests; suite is now 27 passing).
- Figures in `reports/figures/`: eda_* (9) and impact_* (2).
- Config: split dates finalized (comment updated).

**Key results (from `reports/impact.json` and the notebooks)**
- Analysis sample: 94,296 orders with a review; 6,352 late (6.7%).
- Raw gap: on-time 4.29 stars vs late 2.26 (difference -2.02, bootstrap 95% CI [-2.06, -1.98]);
  1-2 star reviews 62.6% of late vs 9.3% of on-time orders.
- Adjusted (controls, seller-clustered SEs): -1.97 stars, CI [-2.02, -1.92].
- Within-seller (fixed effects): -1.98, CI [-2.03, -1.93] (84,243 orders, 1,199 sellers).
- Ordered logit odds ratio for late: 0.068, CI [0.063, 0.074] (n = 40,000 subsample).
- Dose-response: 4.32 stars (>=8 days early) -> 3.28 (1-3 days late) -> 1.67 (8-14 days late),
  flattening afterwards.
- **Review timing finding:** 70.3% of late orders were reviewed before the parcel arrived (mean
  1.65 stars). For reviews answered after delivery the adjusted penalty is only -0.55 stars,
  CI [-0.61, -0.49]. The survey is sent a median 2 days after the PROMISED date for late orders.

**EDA findings (see notebook 01)**
- Late rate 6.9% (imbalanced; always-on-time is 93.1% accurate and useless).
- Monthly late rate swings from 1.2% (Jun 2018) to 19.2% (Mar 2018); CI half-width <= 1.0 pp.
- Median promise 23 days vs median actual 10 days; 74.4% of orders arrive >= 7 days early.
- State spreads similar on both ends (customer 18.5 pp, seller 16.6 pp); distance 4.5% (<100 km)
  to 11.9% (1500-2500 km); same-state 4.5% vs 8.1% inter-state.
- Weekday spread only 1.2 pp; Black Friday window 17.1% vs 9.0% rest of month.
- Splits: train 50,603 orders / 2,852 late (5.6%); validation 20,241 / 2,556 (12.6%);
  test 24,093 / 1,096 (4.5%).

**Mistakes caught and corrected (kept here for honesty)**
1. First sensitivity check used `review_creation_date`, which is when the SURVEY was sent, not when
   the customer answered. Switched to `review_answer_timestamp` and added evidence on survey timing.
2. Several first-draft inferences in notebook 01 were unsupported by the data (e.g. "customer-side
   spread is wider than seller-side", "routes are worse than distance alone explains"); all were
   rewritten after executing and checking the outputs.

**Open items for later phases**
- Validation (12.6% late) vs test (4.5%) shift: expect over-prediction after calibration (Phase 6/7).
- Reviews answered before delivery imply interventions must happen before the promised date (Phase 7/9 policy framing).
- Seller fixed effects remove only time-constant seller quality; time-varying problems remain a threat.

## Phase 6 - Models, tuning, calibration (2026-10-14)

**Goal:** train logistic regression, EBM, LightGBM (+ random forest for comparison), tune on
validation, test class weighting, calibrate - without letting tuning and calibration share data.

**Acceptance (BUILD_PLAN):** `reports/validation_results.json` has all models; a comparison table
is printed; saved models reload and reproduce validation scores.

**Built**
- `models/data.py` - model-ready frames, saved category levels, time sub-splits of validation.
- `models/train.py` - preprocessing (impute + indicators, log + scale, one-hot) and fitters for
  LR, RF, LightGBM (early stopping on PR-AUC), EBM (time-ordered early stopping via `bags`).
- `models/tune.py` - Optuna studies (objective: PR-AUC on `val_tune`); class-weight is a searched
  hyperparameter so the experiment is recorded.
- `models/calibrate.py` - none / sigmoid / isotonic, chosen forward-in-time by Brier.
- `models/run.py` - orchestration, persistence (`models/*.joblib` + metadata), reload check,
  comparison table. `make train` wired; `evaluation/metrics.py` gained a paired bootstrap.
- Tests: `tests/test_models.py` (11). Suite: 78 passing.
- Outputs: `reports/validation_results.json`, `reports/model_comparison.md`, 4 saved models.

**How validation was split (all by time)**
| sub-split | dates | orders | late | late rate | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 2016-09-15..2018-01-31 | 50,603 | 2,852 | 5.6% | fit models |
| val_tune | 02-01..03-15 | 10,057 | 1,784 | 17.7% | tune + early stop |
| calib_fit | 03-16..04-07 | 5,141 | 587 | 11.4% | fit calibrators |
| calib_eval | 04-08..04-30 | 5,043 | 185 | 3.7% | check calibrators |
`val_calib` = calib_fit + calib_eval (10,184 orders, 7.6% late) is never seen by tuning, so it
is the honest out-of-sample column.

**Results (from `reports/validation_results.json`)**
| model | PR-AUC val_tune (tuned on) | PR-AUC val_calib [95% CI] (honest) | ROC-AUC val_calib | lift@10% val_calib | calibrator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prior rate | 0.177 | 0.076 | 0.500 | - | - |
| rule | 0.230 | 0.102 | 0.627 | - | - |
| logreg | 0.372 | **0.174** [0.157, 0.196] | 0.716 | 3.06 | none |
| random forest | 0.360 | 0.126 [0.113, 0.144] | 0.595 | 2.19 | none |
| lightgbm | 0.393 | 0.137 [0.122, 0.159] | 0.621 | 2.44 | sigmoid |
| ebm | **0.395** | 0.122 [0.109, 0.139] | 0.592 | 2.38 | sigmoid |
- Base rate on val_calib is 7.6%, so logistic regression is ~2.3x better than guessing in
  PR-AUC and ~3x in lift at 10% capacity; the rule baseline is 0.102.
- Paired PR-AUC differences on val_calib: LR - LightGBM = +0.037 [+0.024, +0.050];
  EBM - LightGBM = -0.015 [-0.030, -0.002]; EBM - LR = -0.052 [-0.068, -0.037].
- Class weighting: LR - unweighted better (best 0.372 vs 0.359 balanced). LightGBM - `balanced`
  won the search (0.393 vs 0.374 unweighted) but TPE concentrated trials (42 balanced vs 3
  unweighted), so this is weak evidence. A balanced model's raw probabilities are unusable
  (calib_eval Brier 0.435) until calibrated (0.043).
- Chosen hyperparameters are in the JSON (LR C = 50.4 near the top of the 0.001..100 range).
- Reload check: all four models reproduce validation scores (max abs diff <= 1e-16).
- Two full runs produced identical metrics for LR, LightGBM and EBM; the random forest differs
  only at ~1e-17 (parallel float summation).

**Findings that matter (read these)**
1. **Ranking skill is regime-dependent.** EBM/LightGBM lead on val_tune (the Feb-Mar surge they
   were tuned on) but fall behind logistic regression on the calmer val_calib. Part of the
   val_tune advantage is tuning optimism; part may be real surge-specific structure.
2. **Calibration on the past is hostage to the regime.** On calib_eval (3.7% late) no model's
   Brier beats the constant train rate (0.0357): LR 0.038, RF 0.042, LightGBM 0.043, EBM 0.045.
   Calibrators fit on calib_fit (11.4% late) push probabilities up just as the rate fell.
   For production this means: recalibrate on a rolling recent window; trust ranking more than
   absolute probability, and say so in the app.
3. **Test will likely over-predict:** test is 4.5% late vs 7.6% on val_calib, the data the
   deployed calibrators were fitted on. Phase 7 will report this and a monthly-recalibrated view.

**Deliberate non-actions (to keep the validation hold-out honest)**
- I did not re-tune after seeing val_calib results. Doing so would contaminate the only
  untouched validation slice. See open decision below.
- Final models are fit on train only (not refit on train + validation).

**Open decision for the owner:** re-tune with expanding-window time-series CV over train + val_tune
(more regimes in the objective, rule 3-compliant). It would likely make hyperparameters less
surge-specific, but it uses val_calib's lesson, so val_calib would stop being fully clean.

## Phase 7 - Final evaluation on the test set (2026-10-14)

**Goal:** evaluate every model on the test set once, with intervals, and value the policy.

**Acceptance (BUILD_PLAN):** `make evaluate` regenerates everything deterministically;
`results.md` references every figure; glass-box vs black-box gap stated with CI.

**Protocol (fixed BEFORE test was opened; commit 8c95fb6)**
1. Primary model by pre-registered rule: best PR-AUC on val_calib -> logistic regression.
2. Entire evaluation code was developed and debugged on the VALIDATION split (`--dry-run`,
   written to the git-ignored `reports/dryrun/`); test rows were not read until the final run.
3. Test unlocked only inside the evaluation stage (`final_evaluation=True`). Run once.

**Built**
- `evaluation/calibration.py` (reliability table, ECE, rolling recalibration using only
  outcomes delivered before each month), `evaluation/slices.py`, `evaluation/policy.py`
  (capacity x intervention-success simulation vs random, rule and an oracle bound),
  `evaluation/plots.py` (5 figures), `evaluation/report.py` (results.md from results.json),
  `evaluation/run.py` (orchestration). `make evaluate`; `--dry-run` flag.
- Outputs: `reports/results.json`, `reports/results.md`, `reports/slices.csv`,
  `reports/policy_simulation.csv`, `reports/error_analysis_<model>.csv` (x4), 5 figures
  `reports/figures/test_*.png`.
- Tests: `tests/test_evaluation.py` (7). Suite: 85 passing.

**Results (TEST, 24,093 orders approved 2018-05-01..08-18, 1,096 late = 4.5%; all from results.json)**
| model | PR-AUC [95% CI] | ROC-AUC | Brier | lift@10% [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| guess training rate | 0.045 [0.043, 0.048] | 0.500 | 0.0435 | 1.03 [0.89, 1.17] |
| hand-written rule | 0.048 [0.045, 0.051] | 0.527 | 0.0436 | 0.94 [0.75, 1.15] |
| **logistic regression** | **0.093 [0.085, 0.105]** | 0.701 | 0.0435 | **2.20 [1.85, 2.62]** |
| random forest | 0.075 [0.069, 0.083] | 0.665 | 0.0462 | 1.63 [1.38, 1.89] |
| LightGBM | 0.078 [0.072, 0.086] | 0.683 | 0.0428 | 1.96 [1.66, 2.28] |
| EBM | 0.074 [0.068, 0.082] | 0.656 | 0.0430 | 1.64 [1.36, 1.96] |

- **Glass-box vs black-box:** EBM - LightGBM = -0.004 PR-AUC, 95% CI [-0.009, +0.002]: no
  reliable difference. LR - LightGBM = +0.015 [+0.009, +0.024]; EBM - LR = -0.019 [-0.028, -0.012].
  Verdict: no accuracy is given up by choosing the glass-box EBM over LightGBM; and simplest
  (logistic regression) is best here.
- **Hand-written rule is no better than guessing on test** (PR-AUC 0.048 vs base rate 0.045,
  lift 0.94): its train-period signal did not survive the regime change.
- **Calibration:** deployed calibrators are decent in the low/mid range (ECE 0.011-0.015 for LR,
  LightGBM, EBM) but over-predict at the top end (e.g. LR bins predicted ~19% vs ~11%
  observed). Brier is no better than a constant (0.0435 vs 0.0434) but log loss improves
  (LR 0.177 vs 0.186). The random forest is clearly mis-calibrated (ECE 0.058).
- **Rolling monthly recalibration did NOT help on test** (LR Brier 0.0454 vs 0.0435 deployed),
  although it helped on the validation dry run. Monthly late rate swings 1.2% - 7.5% and a
  trailing 60-day window lags those swings.
- **Drift:** primary-model ROC-AUC by month: 0.746 (May), 0.776 (Jun), 0.639 (Jul), 0.566
  (Aug); lift@10% 2.83, 3.16, 2.58, 1.07. Ranking is useful for ~2 months after training
  data ends and degrades afterwards. EBM falls below random lift in August (0.83).
- **Weak slices (primary):** customer region NE (ROC-AUC 0.595) and S (0.626), and months
  2018-07 / 2018-08.
- **Policy (primary, simulation):** at 10% daily capacity and intervention success p = 30%,
  about 74 late orders prevented (6.7% of all late) vs about 33 under random selection:
  gain +40 [+30, +52]; the capacity-limited upper bound is 306. At 20% and p = 50%: 208
  (19.0%) vs 110 random. The rule baseline is indistinguishable from random.
- **Error analysis (LR top-20):** false negatives are mostly short São Paulo->São Paulo
  shipments (median 231 km, ~20-day promise) that took ~26 days: late for reasons our
  features cannot see (p about 0.01). False positives are long routes into AL/MA/RJ scored
  p about 0.47 that arrived in ~12 days.

**What this means**
- The system adds real, measurable value for ranking (2.2x lift at 10% capacity) but it is a
  modest tool: most late orders remain unpredictable from approval-time information.
- Predicted probabilities should be shown as relative risk bands, and models need scheduled
  retraining/monitoring (drift stretch phase S2 is justified by this result).

was tested and not supported; reported as such.

## Phase 8 - Explainability (2026-10-14)

**Goal:** global and per-order explanations in plain English, with an agreement check.

**Acceptance (BUILD_PLAN):** template coverage test passes; reason codes read naturally (owner
reviews 10); agreement report exists.

**Built**
- `explain/contributions.py` - per-order, per-feature log-odds contributions for LR
  (coefficient x preprocessed value, mapped back to source features), EBM (term scores; an
  interaction is split equally between its two features) and LightGBM (TreeSHAP). Each satisfies
  `sum(contributions) + intercept == model log-odds`.
- `explain/reason_codes.py` + `configs/reason_templates.yaml` - 16 reason groups, wording in one
  reviewable file; top 3 "adds risk" and top 2 "reduces risk" above 0.05 log-odds.
- `explain/ebm_explain.py` (importances, shape functions, interaction heatmap),
  `explain/shap_explain.py` (beeswarm, bar, dependence, waterfall), `explain/agreement.py`
  (rank and direction agreement, marginal direction), `explain/cases.py`, `explain/run.py`.
- `make explain` (12 s). `notebooks/04_explainability.ipynb` (27 cells, 3 case studies).
- Tests: `tests/test_explain.py` (12). Suite: 97 passing.
- Outputs: `reports/test_reason_codes.parquet` (72,279 rows = 24,093 test orders x 3 models),
  `reports/reason_code_samples.md` (10 for review), `reports/explain_agreement.md`,
  `explain_summary.json`, `global_importance_{features,groups}.csv`, `ebm_term_importance.csv`,
  `explain_direction_agreement.csv`, 6 `explain_*.png` + 3 case waterfalls.

**Results (validation set for global analyses; test set for reason codes)**
- Exactness: additive-identity max error LR 3.6e-15, LightGBM 7.1e-15, EBM 3.1e-15.
- Every test order receives at least one risk factor from each of the three models (100%).
- Rank agreement of global importance: LightGBM vs EBM Spearman 0.78 (top-10 overlap 80%);
  LR vs LightGBM 0.41 (30%); LR vs EBM 0.40 (50%).
- Top reason groups: LightGBM and EBM both: route history, seller record, distance. LR:
  payment, same state, approval timing (a much flatter profile).
- Direction: of 32 numeric features with a clear direction in at least two models, 26 agree and
  6 conflict (geo_imputed, handling_window_days, days_to_next_holiday, freight_ratio,
  total_weight_kg, seller_n_past); 4 differ from the one-feature-at-a-time direction
  (approve_month, days_to_next_holiday, holidays_in_promise_window, freight_ratio).
- EBM: main effects carry 72.3% of importance, 10 interactions 27.7%; strongest
  `approve_month x days_to_next_holiday` (7.6% of total).

**Problems found and fixed during the phase (kept for honesty)**
1. First template draft used judgement words ("elevated", "tight", "weak"). The agreement table
   showed `global_late_rate_30d` has direction "higher -> LESS risk" in LR and LightGBM, so a
   positive contribution would print "Network-wide delays are elevated: 2% late" - a sentence
   contradicting its own number. Wording rule changed to facts + sign-driven suffix (D-062).
2. Displayed facts did not match drivers: the calendar sentence mentioned holidays in window
   while `days_to_next_holiday` drove the score; the timing sentence omitted `approve_month`.
   Both sentences now include the driving fields.
3. NaN values printed as "nan" (format spec never raised); now render as "n/a" (tested).
4. Marginal-direction threshold of 0.2 reported "no differences" because single features are
   individually weak; lowered to 0.03 (nonzero at n = 20k).

**Findings that matter**
- Linear and tree models give the same order very different probabilities (case 1: LR 62%,
  LightGBM 12%, EBM 14%): LR's top end is overconfident (consistent with the Phase 7 calibration
  plot). Display risk bands and rank, not exact percentages.
- Calendar proxies (`approve_month`, holiday features) are used by every model and show up in
  reason codes ("approved on a Friday in August (reduces risk)"). They mostly encode which part
  of the year the training data covered. Candidate for removal in a v2 (needs a fresh
  validation cycle; not done now because test has been opened).
- LR leans on groups tree models ignore (payment 0.48 vs 0.007; network delays 0.33 vs 0.035)
  yet was the best out-of-time ranker. Hypothesis (unproven): broad use of many stable features
  transfers better than heavy reliance on history features whose behaviour shifted.

**Case studies (mechanical selection by primary-model score; test set)**
- True positive: late (15.2 days vs 6.7 promised); all three models lead with route history.
- False positive: on time (11.8 vs 18.1 promised) but LR 62%; LightGBM 11%, EBM 8%. Reasons were
  real facts (long route, route history, thin seller record).
- False negative: late (36.2 days vs 27.3) with all models below 3%: short same-state route,
  established seller, low-risk route - nothing at approval time pointed to it.
